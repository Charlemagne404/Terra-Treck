# Core Identity

This game is a persistent long-term city/settlement builder powered by the player's real-world movement.

The core fantasy is:

> “This city exists because of my real-life movement.”

Walking in real life generates the resources, labor, momentum, or currency needed to grow the city.

The walking system is the engine.
The city-building experience is the focus.

This is NOT:
- a fitness app with decorations
- a productivity tracker
- a hardcore city simulator
- an idle game clone
- a hyper-competitive optimization game

The game should feel personal, calming, rewarding, and alive.

---

# Core Emotional Goal

The player should eventually feel:

> “I built this city through months of my real-life movement.”

The city should slowly become:
- a memory archive
- a reflection of routines
- a reflection of consistency
- a long-term personal project

The city should feel emotionally meaningful.

Players should remember:
- old districts
- their first buildings
- periods where they walked a lot
- landmarks unlocked during certain times in life
- expansions tied to routines, vacations, seasons, or habits

The city is meant to become something the player grows attached to over time.

# Main Design Pillars

## 1. Persistent Long-Term Growth

The city should evolve over weeks and months.

This game is about:
- gradual expansion
- steady progression
- long-term attachment
- daily interaction
- visible evolution

The player should feel rewarded for consistency over time.

The city should always feel:
- unfinished
- expandable
- alive
- growing

---

## 2. Relaxing & Low Pressure

The game should feel calming and rewarding.

Avoid:
- excessive punishment
- stressful mechanics
- time pressure
- fear of missing out
- aggressive optimization
- competitive pressure
- grind-heavy design

The game should encourage:
- daily check-ins
- relaxed play sessions
- personal creativity
- slow progression
- emotional attachment

The player should WANT to come back.

---

## 3. Visual Progression Matters

Visual city growth is one of the most important feelings in the game.

The city should visibly evolve through:
- denser districts
- upgraded roads
- more advanced buildings
- landmarks
- parks
- decoration
- environmental detail
- lighting
- population/activity

The player should easily notice the difference between:
- a one-week city
- a one-month city
- a one-year city

Growth should feel satisfying.

---

## 4. City-Builder First

This is primarily a city/settlement building game.

The walking mechanic supports the building loop.

Core gameplay should focus on:
- placing buildings
- upgrading structures
- expanding districts
- improving infrastructure
- shaping the city layout
- unlocking new architecture
- personalization

The game should NOT revolve around:
- exercise statistics
- calorie tracking
- fitness metrics
- productivity systems

Walking exists to fuel the city's growth.

# City Evolution Philosophy

The city should evolve naturally over time.

The progression should feel like:
> “a small settlement gradually modernized into a thriving city.”

NOT:
> “the player unlocked a new era.”

Avoid hard civilization-style era jumps.

Instead, use:
- organic urban growth
- layered architecture
- historical districts
- gradual modernization

Example progression:

## Early Game
- dirt roads
- tiny homes
- wooden structures
- farms
- village centers
- lanterns
- workshops

## Mid Game
- brick buildings
- paved roads
- markets
- schools
- row houses
- parks
- larger districts

## Late Game
- plazas
- apartments
- modern infrastructure
- office buildings
- transit systems
- dense urban centers
- landmarks

Old architecture should remain relevant.

The city should feel layered and historic.

Players should be encouraged to preserve older districts instead of replacing everything.

# Player Loop

Core gameplay loop:

1. Player walks in real life
2. Health app syncs step count
3. Steps become usable city-building currency/resources
4. Player expands or upgrades city
5. City visually evolves
6. New buildings/features unlock
7. Player becomes more attached to city
8. Repeat daily over long periods of time

The loop should remain satisfying even in short sessions.

Population should feel important.

- new building categories unlock
- architecture modernizes
- districts expand
- infrastructure improves

Possible city stages:

| Population | Stage |
|---|---|
| 0–100 | Settlement |
| 100–500 | Village |
| 500–2k | Town |
| 2k–10k | City |
| 10k+ | Metropolis |

These should feel like natural development milestones, not hard “level ups.”
